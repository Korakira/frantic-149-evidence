# Investment software: compare the workflow, not the AI label

*Korakira research sample · Sources reviewed 16 November 2026*

Investment software is not one interchangeable category. A platform that maintains investment records, an interface to portfolio analytics, and an assistant that prepares research can all advertise AI while solving different problems. This five-product comparison illustrates how to separate documented scope from assumptions. It uses public primary sources; no products were installed or tested.

**Aladdin — an investment platform with AI features.** BlackRock describes Aladdin as connecting the investment process through shared data across public and private markets. Its site also lists Aladdin Copilot. This makes a simple “traditional software versus AI” split misleading: an established platform can contain AI capabilities. The material supports a broad investment-platform classification, but does not establish implementation cost, model accuracy or suitability for a particular organisation. [BlackRock product overview](https://www.blackrock.com/aladdin).

**SimCorp One — an integrated operational suite.** SimCorp describes a common data foundation extending from the front office to accounting, including public and private assets. This is a stronger indication of operational scope than a generic promise to accelerate research. However, statements about real-time updates and fewer reconciliation delays remain vendor claims here. A purchasing assessment would need evidence about the required accounting workflows, integrations and service commitments. [SimCorp One overview](https://www.simcorp.com/solutions/simcorp-one).

**FactSet Portfolio Analytics MCP — an additional access layer.** FactSet's announcement of 26 June 2026 describes a limited release that exposes existing, pre-calculated portfolio analytics through conversational and agent interfaces. Performance, attribution and risk outputs are the focus. It should therefore be compared as a way to access governed analytics, rather than assumed to replace an entire investment-management system. The announcement alone does not establish general availability or access for every customer. [FactSet announcement](https://investor.factset.com/news-releases/news-release-details/factset-expands-model-context-protocol-suite-portfolio-analytics).

**Finster AI — research and financial workflow assistance.** Finster describes financial analysis, workflow automation, source citations and organisation-level controls. Its website identifies asset management among the markets it serves. These statements support examining it as an AI-focused research and workflow tool. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate a complete investment accounting system, order-management system or authoritative portfolio record. Those capabilities should remain unconfirmed in a comparison unless specific evidence is found. [Finster product overview](https://www.finster.ai/).

**Rogo — agents and connected financial workflows.** Rogo presents financial agents, connections to organisational data and controls for institutional use. That places its stated proposition around completing financial work with AI and shared context. It does not automatically make the product equivalent to an accounting suite. Customer counts and productivity claims would also require careful attribution; they are not independent measures of market share or performance in this sample. [Rogo product overview](https://rogo.com/).

**A useful comparison records three things:** the workflow being supported, the evidence for the claimed capability, and what remains unknown. For these products, comparable pricing, implementation effort, actual output accuracy and coverage of a specific institution's requirements have not been established. A practical next step would be to define one task and evaluate each relevant product against the same inputs and acceptance checks. The research conclusion is about product categories and evidence quality, not a recommendation to buy a particular system.
